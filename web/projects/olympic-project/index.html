Olympic Games Data Analysis & Visualization (1896–2022)

Tools: Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Plotly, GeoPandas GitHub Repository

Project Overview

A comprehensive analysis of Olympic Games data spanning 126 years, uncovering patterns in hosting trends, medal distributions, and national performances. This project reveals which countries dominate the Olympics and how hosting impacts performance.

Key Questions Answered

  • Which countries/continents hosted the most Olympics?
  • How does hosting impact a nation's medal tally?
  • How did France perform across different sports?
  • Which countries won the most medals overall?

Key Findings & Visualizations

1. Olympic Hosting Distribution

Key Insight: Europe has hosted 60% of all Olympic Games, while the United States alone has hosted 15% of all Olympics (8 times). Developing regions like Africa have never hosted.

This uneven distribution suggests that hosting the Olympics requires significant infrastructure and resources that are concentrated in developed nations.

2. Medal Distribution by Country

Top Performers: The United States leads with the most total medals, followed by Germany, UK, France, and the former USSR.

The interactive visualization allows users to explore medal counts by type (gold, silver, bronze) and reveals consistent patterns of dominance by certain nations throughout Olympic history.

3. France's Olympic Performance by Sport

French Strengths: France excels in Athletics (14% of their total medals), Artistic Gymnastics, and Swimming. They've shown decline in some traditional sports.

This detailed breakdown helps understand where France has historically invested in sports development and where opportunities for improvement exist.

4. The Home Advantage Effect

Clear Pattern: Host nations typically win 15-25% more medals during their hosted Games compared to previous Olympics.

Notable examples include the US in 1904 (+200 medals) and 1984 (+170 medals), and France in 1900 (+100 medals). This "home advantage" likely comes from factors like crowd support, familiarity with venues, and increased funding.

Why This Matters

This analysis reveals important patterns in Olympic history that go beyond sports:

  • The persistent dominance of certain nations points to systemic advantages in sports development
  • The hosting imbalance highlights global inequalities in infrastructure and resources
  • The home advantage effect shows how local support and investment can boost performance
  • National sport profiles (like France's fencing strength) reflect cultural sporting traditions

These insights could help future host cities plan their strategies and help nations identify where to focus their athletic development programs.
